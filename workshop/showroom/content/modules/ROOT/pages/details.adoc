== Your credentials

Use this account for both the Keycloak administration console and the
OpenShift Console. Keep the password to yourself.

[cols="1,2",options="header"]
|===
|Item |Value
|Username |`{user}`
|Password |`{password}`
|Keycloak administration console |link:{keycloak_origin}/admin/master/console/[{keycloak_origin}/admin/master/console/,window=_blank]
|OpenShift Console |link:{openshift_console_url}[{openshift_console_url},window=_blank] (sign in on the page titled **Sign in to sso**)

Open both consoles in their own browser tabs. The OpenShift Console cannot
be shown inside this guide's split view: its tab there opens the Console in a
new browser tab and leaves an error in the pane, which you can ignore. The **Terminal** tab in the split view is a
shell with `curl` and `jq`; only the electives use it. In Module 2 you also create two
lab users, `reader` and `manager`, with passwords you choose. Write them down;
you need them from Module 3 on.

== What is already prepared

* A shared Keycloak server, where your account can create a realm and
  administer only the realms it creates.
* Your OpenShift namespace with the SPA frontend, BFF, proxy (and its Redis
  session store), three APIs, and the `reports` app for Module 8 already
  deployed. The BFF, proxy, and `reports` wait for the realm you create.
* ConfigMaps for the SPA, proxy, and APIs already set to your realm's issuer.
  You connect the BFF yourself in Module 4. Four Secrets hold a placeholder
  until you paste the client secrets: three in Module 4 and one in Module 8.
* Cluster logging and distributed tracing. Your applications send logs and
  traces automatically; you read them in the OpenShift Console under
  **Observe → Logs** and **Observe → Traces**. Both show only your own data:
  logs from your namespace, and traces in your own tenant, `{attendee_id}`.

== Stop conditions

Pause and contact the facilitator if your namespace is not the one assigned
to you, the realm name is already owned by someone else, an app URL belongs to
another attendee, or the Keycloak user interface (UI) offers server-wide administration. Do not
try to fix shared cluster or Keycloak settings yourself.
